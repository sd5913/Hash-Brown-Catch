# Design direction and decisions

The intended audience is casual players who enjoy short, cute browser games, with same-screen play as an additional social option in the current prototype.

The selected visual direction is Crunch Club: a red-and-cream American breakfast diner, warm golden highlights and elongated hash browns. Character faces remain uncluttered, with broad highlights and a few toasted edges. Earlier dense flecks were simplified in response to the visual critique that they felt unpleasant and crowded.

Caught hash browns remain visible in the basket and on a serving plate. Missed potatoes become mash and drifting ghosts. This makes success and failure visible and gives the game a playful identity.

## Replay motivation

The team identified that a round-only score offered limited long-term motivation. The wallet and starter shop separate earned coins from score penalties and let players unlock cosmetic items across rounds. The current update also explores photo-to-pixel characters, local rankings and same-screen rivalry/cooperation. These are implemented prototype features; retention or enjoyment benefits still need player evidence.

## Definition of Done for the current handoff

- Another person can launch the game using the README.
- Input, catches, misses, hazards, pause and replay behave as described.
- Wallet and cosmetics persist on reload where local storage is available.
- Local leaderboard examples are clearly labeled as house targets.
- A non-team player attempts the core action without coaching; anonymous observations and one resulting change are recorded.

Automated checks can support the first conditions but do not complete the final playtest condition.
